Step 2 of 3 · Shade

Does anything block the sun?

Buildings, trees and tanks shade a panel at certain times of the day and year. Trace their outline and the app checks it against the sun's path.

  1. Stand where the panel is, or where it will go.
  2. Tap Camera on, then Trace skyline.
  3. Turn slowly in a full circle, keeping the crosshair on the top edge of obstacles (or on the horizon).
  4. Tap Stop tracing when the bar reaches 360°.

How the numbers are calculated

Orientation. The phone's orientation angles (α, β, γ) give a rotation matrix. Its third column is the screen's normal vector in East–North–Up coordinates. Flat on a panel, that vector is the panel normal. Upright, its reverse is the camera's line of sight.

n = Rz(α)·Rx(β)·Ry(γ)·[0,0,1]ᵀ   tilt = acos(n_up)   azimuth = atan2(n_east, n_north)

Sun position. For every 3rd day of the year at 10-minute steps of solar time, from the declination δ and hour angle ω at latitude φ:

sin(elev) = sinφ·sinδ + cosφ·cosδ·cosω   δ = 23.45°·sin(360°·(284+n)/365)

Clear-sky irradiance. Kasten–Young air mass, then Meinel's beam model. Diffuse light is taken as 10% of beam.

DNI = 1361·0.7^(AM^0.678) W/m²

Plane-of-array irradiance uses the isotropic-sky model. Beam light counts only when the sun is above the measured skyline. Sky diffuse is scaled by the sky-view factor of the skyline.

G = DNI·max(0, cos θ)·[sun visible] + DHI·(1+cos β)/2·SVF + GHI·ρ·(1−cos β)/2

Energy. kWh/yr = kWp × annual G (kWh/m²) × performance ratio × 0.74. The 0.74 factor accounts for clouds and haze and brings clear-sky totals in line with typical Indian yields of about 1,450–1,550 kWh per kWp. Percentage losses do not depend on this factor.

Limits. This is a single clear-sky model with no local weather data, so it does not capture the monsoon. Treat absolute kWh as an estimate and the percentages as the reliable part.
